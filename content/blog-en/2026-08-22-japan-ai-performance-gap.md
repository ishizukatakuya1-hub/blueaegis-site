Japan is often said to be behind on AI. That is not quite right. What is behind is not adoption. It is results.

The PwC Japan Group's "Generative AI survey 2026 Spring", published in June 2026, compares six countries — Japan, the United States, the United Kingdom, China, Germany and South Korea. In Japan it surveyed 932 respondents at section-manager level or above, at companies with revenue of 50 billion yen or more.

On adoption, Japan reports 87%. Against 90% in the United States and 89% in the United Kingdom, that is no longer a meaningful gap.

On outcomes it is a different picture. Only 9% of Japanese companies said generative AI had produced results that far exceeded expectations, against 38% in the United States and 32% in the United Kingdom — last among the six. On tying AI to financial results, Japan reports 40%, close to half the 75% in the United States and 74% in the United Kingdom.

## The gap between "we use it" and "it works"

This shape is not confined to companies. It describes individual practice just as well. You have built the habit of opening the tool. You have it draft the occasional piece of writing. But you cannot point to where it shows up in your own results — leaving earlier, better quality, work you would not otherwise have won. That state, using it without much to show, is what the Japanese 9% is made of.

Closing the gap means lifting the purpose of the work one level: from substituting for a task to redefining what the result is.

## Three ways an individual can lift it

First, measure the output rather than the time. Do not stop at "the email was quicker." Ask whether the time saved bought you one more proposal.

Second, use AI as something to think against, not only to write with. "Give me three weaknesses in this plan." "Argue against it from the customer's side." These change the quality of the work itself, which drafting alone does not.

Third, pass on what worked. What the survey describes is also a picture of individual ingenuity that never reaches the organisation. Turning your own prompts and procedures into a single page and handing it round moves you from someone who can use AI to someone who can spread its use.

## In short

Level on adoption, left behind on results. That is where Japan stands in 2026. Read the other way, it means a great deal of headroom remains for anyone who learns to connect the two. Start this week by asking AI to think with you once, rather than to work for you.
