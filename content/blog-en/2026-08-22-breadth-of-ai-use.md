Not how much you use AI, but how many kinds of work you use it for. New figures from the United States suggest that is where the difference sits.

Gallup's July 2026 release, based on a May 2026 survey of 22,573 US workers, finds that 52% now use AI at work: 30% at least a few times a week, 15% every day. The leading uses are writing and editing (51%), search and research (49%), and general problem solving (39%).

The striking number is the one on perceived benefit. Among people with one or two use cases, 45% report a positive effect on their productivity. Among people with seven or more, 90% do. Between two people who both "use AI", the breadth of use doubles the reported effect.

## Why breadth is what pays

The reason is plain enough. The more uses you have, the faster and more accurately you judge whether to reach for AI at all. Someone who only writes with it will not think of it when they are stuck on a calculation or a piece of research. Someone who has tried it widely recognises, the moment a new task appears, whether it is a job for AI or a job for themselves. What governs productivity is not the capability of the tool but the number of situations in which you remember it exists.

## A realistic order for widening

There is no need to add uses at random. Adding one a week, in rough order of how easily the benefit shows, works well.

1. Writing and editing — emails, first drafts of reports
2. Summarising — long documents or minutes down to three points
3. Search and research — for getting the shape of a subject. Confirm anything that matters against the primary source
4. Generating options — a sounding board that widens the field
5. Tables and data — paste in, reformat, classify
6. Learning — picking up new domain knowledge through dialogue
7. Checking — having your own writing examined for errors and gaps in the argument

By the time those seven are in place, you are no longer someone who uses AI occasionally. You are someone who remembers it at the right moments.

## Organisations are moving too

In the same survey, 47% said their employer had adopted AI tools, up six points from 41% the previous quarter. The environment is being built out steadily. What is in question is whether individual readiness keeps pace.

## In short

Add one new use this week. Summarising is the one to start with. Take a single long document and have it reduced to three lines — that is where breadth begins.
